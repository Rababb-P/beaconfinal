**As the weather becomes colder, I start to wonder how Canada’s arctic animals survive,** and even thrive in the freezing cold, where temperatures regularly dip below -20 degrees, while I can barely handle negative 10!  

  
Arctic animals are quite chubby. This layer of extra fat is called blubber, and acts as insulation to help animals retain heat.  

  

Many sea dwellers, like beluga whales, that live in the frigid arctic waters, and fluffy arctic hares have blubber.  

  

Arctic animals are also often quite round. Have you ever noticed that arctic hares tend to tuck themselves into little balls of fur during storms? Compared to other leporids, they also have shorter faces and ears. A round body is optimal for heat conservation because it provides the least surface area compared to volume of an animal. Less surface area means there is less space for heat to escape.  

  

Skin and fur colour are another factor that contributes to arctic survival. Many arctic animals like polar bears and arctic hares are white, especially in the winter. This helps them camouflage in the snow, to avoid being spotted. However, arctic animals also benefit from being coloured black. Arctic hares have black fur at the tips of their ears, to prevent heat loss and accelerate heat gain.  

  

Although these adaptations work quite well for Canada's arctic animals, I would much rather stay warm and cozy in the positive 20 degrees of my own house.
