**Throughout my tenure at *The Beacon,* I've established myself as an analytical and highly respected(?) voice.** I’ve delivered some of the most damning critiques in our agency’s 23-year history. But a bold question arises: can I put my money where my mouth is?  

  
Time-honoured favourites like sugar cookies and gingerbread houses are at the heart of Western holiday baking, and it’s unlikely that these classics will be too difficult for amateur bakers. The Bûche de Noël (or Yule Log) is of a much higher caliber. It’s a rolled chocolate cake filled with whipped cream and frosted in chocolate icing, resembling a wooden log. A real showstopper—if executed properly. This is my attempt at baking Bûche de Noël.  
  
If you’ve ever baked from scratch, these processes will be familiar: sift dry ingredients, cream sugar and fat (in this case, egg yolks), and combine. My first humbling came shortly thereafter. Seven eggs were to be separated so the whites could be beaten separately. This comes with the requirement that the egg yolks must remain intact such that no egg yolk drips into the whites, as the yolk fat would make the aeration process much harder.  

  

Despite exercising utmost skill, yolk was cross contaminated. The whites were able to whip ever so slightly, but not to an ideal state. The show had to go on nonetheless, so I reluctantly folded the barely-whipped whites into the batter and baked it for 20 minutes.  
  
After coming out of the oven, I had a short window to roll the cake into a cylinder while it was still warm and malleable. Thankfully, Ms. Wisam undertook that responsibility and used great finesse to roll. During this time, I took on the responsibilities of whipping cream and chocolate frosting, which went sweetly.
Visual art has never been my strong suit and my décor jobs have always been redone and redelegated to somebody better than me, but there’s something fulfilling about frosting your own cake—that’s something AI can never take away from you.  

  

Ultimately, my decoration job looked more like the ‘reality’ side of an Instagram vs. Reality meme.
Students in the Grade 11/12 String Class were subjected to a taste test. Violinist and *Beacon* photographer Daniel Mok described the cake as “like bread.” Double bassist Yusen Lin said “the [frosting] was a little strong but the cakey part and whipped cream were nice.” In a one-on-one interview with my instructor, Ms. Chang, she remarked—with visible apprehension—that “everything tasted great.” Mok later added that “the texture is like solidified cake batter.”  

  
To quote Dance Moms’ Abby Lee Miller: I thought I was stronger than this, but obviously I am not.
