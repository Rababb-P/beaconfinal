**“It’s going to be so much fun.”**  

 
These are actual words quoted from an upperclassman when others inquired about camp. Supposedly, the “best experience at Martingrove,” I was skeptical if everyone hyping it up had joined the alleged cult at camp. After all, there were chants and requirements to all dress the same hue. For all the grade nine readers, this is my personal opinion and casual review of leadership camp, which will either sway you away or towards attending next fall.  

  
What are some pros of camp? Firstly, it’s one of the most welcoming environments I’ve ever been to. When you go to camp, there’s a comforting energy in the air that allows you to express yourself without any judgement. I can remember countless times I’ve embarrassed myself or felt out of place, but at camp, those moments are unheard of.  

  
Additionally, every activity is student-led. Throughout camp, we were led around to a multitude of activities, all by somewhat familiar faces. This adds to the inviting atmosphere and shows how passionate students are about camp. When the activities are looked at from afar, you can certainly tell that they put effort and dedication into it.  

  
Also, Manitou is a wonderful place. Mealtime is one of the best parts of camp. The staff are incredibly hardworking, and the food is enough to fuel you for the day, so you never go hungry. Mealtime is when you “embarrass yourself” the most. Those were the moments I let myself go to bond with others and become extremely spirited. Honestly, camp has already become a highlight of high school. It’s a fun and relaxing week, and an opportunity to meet new people and to get away from school.  

  
However, everything has some cons. The worst one, objectively, is how cold it gets up at camp. This is a statement excessively repeated to campers before attending, and is occasionally not taken seriously. Take. That. Seriously. My first night at camp was one of the worst sleeps of my life. Waking up physically shivering and having to shower in 4° Celsius water is one of the reasons so many people get sick after camp. Leaving my sleeping bag was the hardest part of camp. Manitou really shows off how contrasting Canada’s weather can be. Warm and sunny during the day to freezing at night. It was so cold I could even see my breath.   

  
On a final note, the second most terrible part of camp was leaving. Spending day and night with your peers and friends, stress-free and wild, is a gratifying experience. Returning home and then back to school makes you reminisce about the joys of leadership. If you were to ask me my opinion about attending camp, I’d encourage you excessively. Putting the cold aside, camp is a terrific place with nutritious food, new friends, and exciting activities. Leadership camp is an inclusive space where kids have the time to be kids. Brave the frigid weather and go to camp–it’s worth it!
