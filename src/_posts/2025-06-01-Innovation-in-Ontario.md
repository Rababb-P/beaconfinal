**Innovation isn’t just a buzzword; it’s the driving force behind Ontario’s future.** At DiscoveryX 2025, the province’s premier innovation showcase, it was clear that Ontario isn’t just keeping up with the world; it’s aiming to lead it.  

Held on April 16th and 17th at the Enercare Centre in Toronto, DiscoveryX 2025 brought together over 3,000 attendees, including entrepreneurs, researchers, government leaders, and students. Organized by the Ontario Centre of Innovation (OCI), the event spotlighted new technologies and ideas across a wide range of sectors, from AI and cleantech to life sciences, and automotive mobility. There were over 250 exhibitors, dozens of pitch competitions, and keynote speakers like Geoffrey Hinton (the “Godfather of AI”) and futurist Amy Webb.  

But what made this event truly special wasn’t just the gadgets and prototypes, it was the energy — the belief that Ontario has the potential to build the next global tech giants. And nobody represents that vision better than Claudia Krywiak, President and CEO of OCI.
After attending one of the panel discussions, I had the opportunity to sit down with Ms. Krywiak for a short interview.  



I wanted to understand what she believes gives Ontario an edge in a fiercely competitive global innovation race.  

“There are two things that really distinguish us,” she told me. “One is talent, and this cuts across sectors. In AI, for example, we are a global center of excellence for both research and talent. The same applies to life sciences. Number two is our ecosystem. Whether you’re a student with a bright idea, a university researcher, or an early-stage entrepreneur, there are resources across the province; regional innovation centres, funding programs like OCI, that can help take your idea from concept to market.”  


Her answer was clear: Ontario’s greatest strength isn’t just what we build, it’s who’s building it, and the support around them.  

But no system is perfect. I asked her about what Ontario still needs to improve. She didn’t hesitate: “Access to capital is one area that is a challenge. If we want to build globally competitive companies, especially in deep tech sectors, we need to make sure they have the funding to scale.”  



She’s right. Talent and ideas are essential, but without capital, even the best innovations can get stuck in the lab.
Walking around the DiscoveryX floor, I saw projects that could genuinely change the world. A neuroscience startup from the UofT won the *Ignite Innovation Pitch Competition.* Queen’s-based LenSense Inc. won the “Where’s Next” pitch. Every corner of the event was buzzing with students, startups, and experts sharing their vision for what’s next.  


Events like DiscoveryX remind us that innovation isn’t limited to Silicon Valley. It’s happening right here in Ontario, and people like Claudia Krywiak are making sure the next generation of problem solvers, inventors, and entrepreneurs have the support they need to succeed.  



Whether it’s clean energy, health tech, or quantum computing, Ontario’s innovation ecosystem is growing fast. And as a student interested in science and technology, being there was inspiring. It reminded me that innovation isn’t something you wait for. It’s something you create.
