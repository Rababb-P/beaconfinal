Like millions of social media users, you’ve probably encountered TikTok’s passé ‘performative male’ archetype, alongside a barrage of younger men professing sardonic adoration of feminist literature. As a feminist and avid reader, I find myself wishing this phenomenon was more than a punchline, and that more young people felt inspired to dig deeper into this diverse, complex, and vital canon¹ of feminist writers. Let’s have a look at a few reads that’ll hopefully have you clicking ‘borrow’ as often as the like button on performative male parodies this fall.

   **Feminism Is for Everybody: Passionate Politics - bell hooks**  

     
As the title generously reveals, this is an excellent primer on basic feminist theory. Through compassionate prose that appeals to common sense over ideology, hooks illustrates the importance of advocating for gender equality and social justice. To any hesitant readers—fear not! There is an appreciated absence of impenetrable jargon in this book. Instead, hooks opens a dialogue that argues for feminism as more than a fringe issue and places women’s rights at the forefront.  

  
**Invisible Women - Caroline Criado Perez**  

  
Calling all non-fiction lovers! This meticulously researched work exposes just how deep gender biases run even in supposedly objective data analysis. Arguing that we live in a “world built largely for and by men,” Criado-Perez uncovers how women are often excluded from crucial research, medical studies, and even life-saving designs. Engrossing exposé!  

  
  **Honourable Mentions:**  

    
*Sister Outsider* (Audre Lorde), *The Second Sex* (Simone de Beauvoir), *Blood* (Dr. Jen Gunter), and *Of Woman Born* (Adrienne Rich).  

  
  The power of feminist literature lies in its defiance of genre; if dense theory and social critique aren’t your cup of tea, there will always be a poetry collection or fantasy novel full of richly developed female characters and feminist themes waiting to be discovered. In reframing our perception of these books, we stand to gain a deeper understanding of the ongoing struggle for women’s liberation, free of hesitation in the face of texts that may seem “too academic” for the everyday reader. And for anyone worried about seeming performative—remember that reading benefits only the mind, and that enlightenment will always serve you where social clout fails.
