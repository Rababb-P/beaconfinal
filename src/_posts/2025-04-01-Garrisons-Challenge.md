**It’s not every day that you hear about someone attempting to swim across the English Channel**, but Mr. Garrison, one of the many great teachers at Martingrove, is preparing for an
intense and difficult challenge: a 33-kilometer swimming marathon.  

This coming July, Mr. Garrison will embark on a 12-16 hour solo swim from Dover, UK,  to Calais, France. While the straight-line distance is 33 kilometres, tides and currents may extend it to 40 to 50 kilometres.  

 His boat captain and crew will guide him safely, monitor Mr. Garrison’s health and ensure his nutrition throughout the journey.  

   
Mr. Garrison’s challenge is both compelling and inspiring. All donations
 will support the Daily Bread Food Bank, an organization striving to provide emergency support for families and individuals who are combating food insecurity. Post-COVID, Daily Bread Food Bank saw a “staggering increase in visits as Toronto declared a food insecurity emergency” (“Toronto Declares Food Insecurity an Emergency”).  

 Over the course of three years, Mr. Garrison committed to an intensive regimen, swimming for an hour four times a week with longer, more demanding sessions on weekends. His dedication meant waking up before 5 a.m. to complete two to three hours of training, most mornings before school, exemplifying his perseverance and passion.  


Mr. Garrison describes the past few years of training as nothing short of transformative:  


“It has been a truly life changing experience. I've never done anything this extreme before, so I decided to do a full three years of training.  

Since then, I've had a ton of firsts - it started with my first mile in a pool three years ago, to joining my first ever swim team, and then developed into doing long distance races in open water, including the iconic swim from Alcatraz and an ultramarathon swim around Key West.  


A big part of this has been mental training as well. There are no guarantees, but I just need to work as hard as I can to prepare for the things that I can control while understanding that any number of things can happen that are outside of it.”

Mr. Garrison’s journey and the challenges he has overcome to complete the marathon are truly remarkable. As the marathon approaches, we encourage donations towards his personal Daily Bread fundraiser. If you’re unable to donate, consider spreading awareness about the rise in food insecurity and advocate for lasting policy changes. Let’s wish Mr. Garrison the best in his marathon!
