**As I’m walking down the hallway in a sea of grayscale attire, I start to wonder when the switch really began.** Was it during the pandemic? Was there a mass text sent out? Individuality and showing emotion are qualities that have gone down significantly over the past years—but why? Is the possibility of embarrassment too strong? Is there a certain standard set among our age group? Has social media really corrupted us all?  

  

Being nonchalant means to appear casually calm and relaxed, without displaying emotion, enthusiasm, or interest in anything, and has become today’s normal. I believe that one of the reasons for this cultural shift is the anxious fear of embarrassment or not fitting in. According to the National Institute of Health, during 1990–2021, a 52% raise in anxiety within young adults has rapidly occurred, and is only going up. As we know, youth are extremely impressionable and full of many complex emotions. These feelings can make us moody, judgemental, and dismissive of others if they are acting “strange” or “out of the ordinary".  

  

This harbours and feeds all kinds of insecurities, possibly leading to mental illnesses like depression. 
Nonchalance can be viewed as being cool and not caring about anything or anyone. It is a common trait. From a different perspective, it can be viewed as a covering-up of one’s individuality. Not being true to yourself can be a product of people not being able to be themselves because they feel they might get targeted with hate crimes or be discriminated against. In a world where these odious acts sometimes still seep into our lives, don’t be afraid to express yourself; chances are, others want to as well.
