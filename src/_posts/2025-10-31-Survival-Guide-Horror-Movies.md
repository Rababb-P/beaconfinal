**Last year, we explored famous horror movies that anyone could survive.** To keep you on your toes this year, we’re stepping it up with a guide on how to survive any horror movie, from the slashers to the supernatural.  

   
In every slasher, our main character eventually finds themselves alone, face to face with someone like Ghostface or Michael Myers. If you end up in an empty house or a dark alleyway, don’t wait for the music to start. Run toward the nearest crowd. Never go upstairs, never hide behind a curtain, and above all, never look back. There’s nothing behind you worth dying for.  

  
But what if you do find a crowd and the killer is still on your tail? When running for your life in public, don’t just yell for help. Call out for someone directly. Say, “You in the blue jacket, call 911!” It’s a real psychological trick. People are far more likely to act when they have been personally addressed.  

  
Curiosity is everyone’s worst enemy when it comes to paranormal films. If your clock stops at 3:07 a.m., like in The Conjuring, or if you find cursed tapes in your attic like in Sinister, please don’t investigate.  If you hear weird noises coming from the basement or see shadows, the best thing you could do is pretend it’s nothing. This is not your Sherlock Holmes moment.  

  
One of my favorite subgenres, zombie or apocalypse horror, is hands down one of the hardest to survive. Let’s say you’re stuck outside, like in one of the best movies ever,Train to Busan—what can you do now? Your best move is practicality, not bravery. Find shelter first; open spaces are invitations for disaster. Once you’re safe, stock up on what truly matters: food, water, a first-aid kit, a flashlight, and something to defend yourself with. Keep up with the news and never let your guard down.  

  
Psychological horror is the one you can’t outrun. Movies like Get Out and Black Swan prove that paranoia is the real villain. The best thing you can do is be aware. If something feels off, it probably is. It never hurts to question everyone and everything around you; in horror movies, everyone is probably up to something.  

  
At the end of the day, surviving a horror movie is about paying attention and making smart choices. If anything looks like it’s a bad idea… it probably is.
