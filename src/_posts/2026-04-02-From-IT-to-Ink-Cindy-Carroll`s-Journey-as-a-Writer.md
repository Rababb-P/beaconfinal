**Our Martingrove alumna, Cindy Carroll, has achieved remarkable success as a writer.** I had the amazing chance to interview her about the journey that shaped her into the author she is today.

  

Balancing a career in IT and banking, Ms. Carroll spends her evenings pursuing her true passion: writing. She treasures the moments she can carve out for her stories, especially when unraveling their twists and turns. “I love getting the words on the page,” she explains. The best part, she adds, is typing “The End” and seeing the entire story come together.

  

Ms. Carroll’s passion for writing began around Grade 6 and has since grown “more polished and more detailed over the years. ”Her time at Martingrove Collegiate Institute also helped shape her passion for writing. During her  high school years, Ms. Carroll especially valued the friendships she built and the memories that came with them, from “hanging out with my friends in the cafeteria” to spending time “by the parking lot on the benches.” A creative writing class at Martingrove gave her a new perspective on how stories come together. She was given the opportunity to read books created by her own teacher, making her even more intrigued.

  

Several key moments in Ms. Carroll’s life influenced her path as a writer.. A critical part was when “visiting my cousin during the summer when I was 18 or 19 and there was a typewriter out in the basement. She said she was writing a book. It didn't occur to me that I could actually write a novel. So I started writing a novel.” That experience led to the creation of her first novel, which was a historical romance. Growing up, Ms. Carroll  enjoyed reading Dean Koontz, known for his thrillers and horrors. She “started out writing romance because that's what I was reading at the time. But then stories with paranormal elements started pestering me. Then the thriller ideas started.”

  

Today, writing serves as a huge stress reliever for Ms. Carroll. She pours her bottled-up emotions from the week into her characters and plotlines, creating feelings only humans can capture. In spite of the rise of artificial intelligence, Ms. Carroll’s advice to aspiring writers is simple: “Just write. Figure out your process by experimenting with plotting, pantsing (writing by the seat of your pants). There is no one right way to write a book, short story, or poem. Write what you love, and it’s a lot more fun.”
