**Summer begins as the school year ends.** It’s a time people like to think of as free, fun, and relaxing. But summer does not feel that way anymore. Summer often feels more like a deadline than a vacation. It’s the time when you’re supposed to change your life, become your best self, and return to school in the fall ready and prepared. Reality? You don’t need to have it all mapped out this summer. Most people don’t, in fact, and that’s okay.  

  

  Thanks to social media and “glow-up” culture, summer is no longer for sleeping. It’s now a season to show how much you can reinvent yourself. This could be through intense fitness routines, starting new businesses, reading self-help books, or drastic makeovers. There’s a growing belief that summer is when you redesign yourself.
It’s difficult not to compare yourself with other people when you’re noticing so many people on social media who seem to have it all together — improving their lives, travelling the globe, and creating new things. 
But behind every carefully selected post is a reality that we don’t always observe: no one has it all figured out, and that’s okay.  

  Trying to meet an impossible standard can result in burnout and stress, and the feeling that no matter what you do, it is never good enough.  

    
When you use summer as a time to quickly fix yourself, you may lose sight of what summer is all about. Summer is meant to be a break. A time to relax, reflect, and live more leisurely. It doesn’t have to be sitting on the couch doing nothing, but it does require that you slow down the necessity to always get it right or achieve.  

It’s also important to mention that each person has a unique summer. Some people work full-time, others are travelling, working on their artistic endeavours, or simply taking the time to rest and recharge. None of these choices are better than the rest. You don’t need to hold your journey up to someone’s highlight reel.
We’re in a society that has a tendency to define our productivity as our value. That mindset is telling us what we must be constantly doing in order to justify ourselves. But resting is also productive. It allows your body and mind to rebuild and grow in ways that do not always show.  

  

  This summer, do not consider what you can do. Consider what makes you feel good and centred. Maybe that’s trying something different or maybe it’s doing nothing and being alright with that. There is not a “right” way to spend your summer. You do not have to achieve something huge in order for it to be valuable.  

No one knows it all and no one wishes you to either. Life is not a race to the finish line. It is a lesson, a growth, a fall, and a get-up-again. So, let yourself relax this summer. You can just be.  

You don’t have to come back from summer with an ideal body, a five-year plan, or a different identity. You just have to come back relaxed, refreshed, and a little more aware of who you are. That’s sufficient unto itself.
