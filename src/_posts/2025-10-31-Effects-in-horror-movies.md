**Have you ever wondered how horror movies feel so spine-chilling?** The writing and acting of course contributes the most, but a horror movie is not complete without special effects. Horror movies increasingly use video editing and CGI to create terrifying monsters and illusions, but for much of their history, horror movies have also relied on physical special effects, and still do quite heavily  

  
Music and lighting are important elements in fooling your mind. For example, the lighting may be dark and dreary, but whether the scene feels depressing or creepy all hinges on whether the music is sad or scary. Similarly, even if the music being played is blood-curdling shrieks and scary footsteps, the right lighting also decides whether the scene can be understood as horror or comedy.  

  
Lighting can also be used to create other effects like terrifying shadows of creaky trees or flashing lights.  

  
If you have ever watched a ghost movie and come across a foggy graveyard that rattles your bones, you can thank a smoke machine for that. In fact, you can even create smoke using something as simple as dry ice!  

  
Most horror movies also cannot be complete without buckets of fake blood to make injuries believable. Imagine what a tragedy it would be if a ghost wrote on the bathroom mirror with a sharpie instead of blood! A standard recipe for fake blood used in Hollywood is corn syrup and food colouring.  

  
Another less obvious special effect is manipulating the camera. Many cameras are shaken to make it appear as if something sinister is chasing after the main characters, or simply to make the viewer feel as if they are a part of the chase.  

  
Lastly, any good horror movie makes use of prosthetics and makeup! Not all ghosts are animated on a computer or projected on a wall. When there is an actual actor materializing your nightmares, it has to be believable. Suppose the ghost in question has a mouth that was ripped open? A prosthetic mouth can be created to replicate that!  

  
Special effects in horror movies are not as elaborate as they seem, as long as they are used intelligently. Even a high school student can make a horror movie using these simple tricks! If movie-making is not something that interests you, you can still use these tricks to scare your friends on Halloween, or ‘build bravery’ in eager trick-or-treaters when they come to your house hungry for candy!
