**“I’m good at making people feel comfortable. That’s my superpower.”** Ms. Talosi’s warm smile welcomes me into her cozy lamplit office, the prospect of which inexplicably terrifies many students despite her best efforts to “make it a [comforting] space that doesn’t feel intimidating.” I’m on a mission to discover what goes on behind the scenes at Guidance, and MCI’s newest ACL has graciously accepted my request for an interview. “We’re here to support students[, especially] in terms of pathways. On top of that, we’re constantly monitoring students’ graduation progress,” summarizes just some of the vital work that she does for the school. Apart from paperwork and academic planning, Guidance is also a place you can go if you “need to just get [something] off [your] chest.” Ms. Talosi explains that while she’s not a trained therapist, there is an “increased need” for mental health support in schools, and that she’s always there to check in and talk.  

  
Our conversation turns to barriers that limit this work. Gesturing at a bloated folder that makes my Physics textbook look like light reading, she explains the sheer numerical restraints of her job. “I have just under 400 students in my alpha. Obviously, I can’t get to know [them all] super well.” Limited hours in a day and increasing student needs, coupled with “everything in education always getting cut back,” leave the 4-person Guidance team with a “gazillion things” to do every day. I mention how some students are apprehensive of coming into the office due to its “stressed out aura” and wonder if there’s a way of dissuading those fears. It’s certainly a busy place, but Ms. Talosi insists that “you’re not causing the stress. You have a right to talk to your Guidance counsellor.” While not every request can be accommodated, there is nothing to be afraid of. Especially not her inspirational posters…  

  
“That’s what we do, guys; we crush dreams…” Ms. Talosi’s joke is playful, but the undercurrent, a student body with some misconceptions about the people there to support them, leaves a bittersweet tinge. I share a few sentiments I see circulating at the start of every semester from various students either frustrated with their timetables or anxiously anticipating a course change. While delays in processing forms, difficulties switching courses, and other bureaucratic grievances may seem world-ending to many of us, Ms. Talosi emphasizes that “here, we’re not making a delay happen because we don’t want to help you. [We] processed over 400 course requests and there are 960 kids in the school. It just takes time, right?”  

  
I can’t help but smile when Ms. Talosi earnestly shares the most rewarding aspect of her job: “My soul is fed when I get to work with students… [when] I get to know them.” While Guidance may seem like all business, she always ensures “a little human moment first” and strives to connect with students as people and not just status sheets. So, next time you see your Guidance counsellor in the halls, say hi! And maybe brave a visit to drop off those volunteer hours you’ve tentatively been meaning to hand in… It’s never too late to start building a positive relationship with Ms. Talosi, Ms. Molinari, Mr. Simpson, and Ms. Liei,  MCI’s most supportive superheroes
