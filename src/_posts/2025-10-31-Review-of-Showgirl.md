**Calling all Swifties!** Taylor’s brand new album has topped the charts, launching a whole new era of music. With “The Fate of Ophelia” ranking number 1 though she’s never had 2, Taylor Swift currently dominates the top 12 spots of the Billboard Hot 100 with The Life of a Showgirl’s tracklist. Released at midnight on October 2nd, fans were sleepless in the onyx night listening to her 12th album (though not the one on some Swiftie’s wishlists, but fans were just being a little superstitious), inspired by her relationship with Travis Kelce and including nerdy undertones. Let’s dig deeper into the album and Release Party.  

  The vibes of the album can only be described as a mix between Reputation, Lover, and The Tortured Poets Department, with softer song lyrics in “Father Figure” and “Eldest Daughter” and powerful refrains in “Elizabeth Taylor” and “Cancelled.” My personal biggest disappointment on the album was the title track, “Life of a Showgirl” featuring Sabrina Carpenter. Though it’s been locked inside my memory, the song wasn’t bad,  I was just expecting… more?  It felt a bit like an afterthought of a song, with all the effort going into the big three: “The Fate of Ophelia,” “Elizabeth Taylor,” and “Opalite.”  
    
    
  This is unusual for Taylor, since on most of her other albums, we have great titular songs like “Lover”, “Evermore”, “Red”, and “The Tortured Poets Department.”.  
  
   
   With The Release Party of a Showgirl, Taylor Swift is officially the first person to have a top song, album, and movie all at the same time. The experience of the film was phenomenal. I was caught having too much fun, surrounded by Swifties and to see Taylor on the big screen. However, I’d expected music videos for every song, behind-the-scenes for the writing, and more of the thought that went into this album. Instead, the film mostly focused on the behind-the-scenes for “The Fate of Ophelia” and the lyric videos for all the other songs, which felt like an afterthought. On the other hand, her outfits were stunning–the skirt did fit her–and the thought that went into “The Fate of Ophelia” was extensive. She had different outfits and hairstyles for each era of showgirl, ranging from Ophelia in the form of a Pre-Raphaelite portrait model to Marilyn Monroe as a dancer in a dressing room. Taylor even shared about her nerdy art and literature background, and her love of sourdough!  
   
  
  
  For those who didn’t clue in: in “The Fate of Ophelia”, Taylor compares herself to Ophelia, a character from Shakespeare’s Hamlet who’s spurned and drowns of a broken heart. Taylor, and other artists alluded to in the video were saved from the same fate–rejection from the public eye and disgrace. You could say their friendships were ruined. As well, Taylor has finally found ‘the one,’ Travis Kelce, who saved her from loneliness. This overall theme coordinates with the title track, depicting showgirls’ lives and how the public view affects them.  

  
  
  Her journey through the eras has been an incredibly beautiful timelapse, and I’m a proud supporter of her music. Even if she gets some hate for ‘cash-grabbing’ her fans and her love being pure profit, I can understand her “any publicity is good publicity” message in “Actually Romantic.” For all the time people spend talking about her, good or bad, it’s still publicity! We really didn’t know the life of a showgirl until this album gave us a glimpse of what it’s like.
