**The months drift and sway, like pendulums. They swing their weights back and forth until they crash into February, colliding like passing ships into a celebration of love, the occasional awkward confession, candy grams, and friendships. Valentine’s is here, and I think that this year, its collision is whatever you want it to be.**  

  

Before it was the whole world’s to define, Valentine’s Day belonged to the Ancient Romans. The exact origins of Valentine’s day are shrouded in mystery, but because of their similarities, it is said to have roots in the ancient pagan celebration of Lupercalia, a fertility festival that the Roman people dedicated to Faunus, the Roman god of agriculture, as well as the first Roman kings, Romulus and Remus.  

  

In a sacred cave, an order of Roman priests would gather to sacrifice a goat for fertility, and a dog for purity. They would skin the goat’s hide into strips, drench them in sacrificial blood, and take to the streets, slapping women and crop fields with the strips in hand. By evening, women would place their names in a big urn and wait for bachelors to pull their name out of it,  often resulting in a marriage between matched pairs.  

  

Apart from a somewhat loose similarity of the colour red, with Valentine’s involving red hearts, roses, etc., and Lupercalia involving… well… blood, the two holidays also take place in mid-February and share connotations of matchmaking and love. Thanks to the Christian Pope Gelasius I, Lupercalia was deemed immoral and was therefore banned, whereas Valentine’s evolved away from this visceral, blood-stained rite into a day more closely associated with romantic love and consumerism. But, it’s not always about romantic love. It’s also not always about other people. You’ll find that in our school community especially, Valentine’s Day has different meanings and significance to everyone.  

  

For Ms. Smart, it’s a day of mixed feelings. She says, “It's great for hanging out with friends, but it's a hard day for aromantic, asexual, and queer people in general for whom Valentine’s might not resonate as much, due to the heteronormativity pushed that day.”  

  

Ms. Chopra says, “It’s a boring day like any other day,” as it’s not a day that is widely celebrated in her culture.  

  

A Grade 12 student says, “It’s a moment to share and give love to people.”  

  

My fellow Grade 11 student, Keira Mak, says her Valentine’s Day involves a routine of “a nice warm shower, warm pajamas, sitting in a bean bag chair with a fluffy blanket and lots of pillows, a cup of tea, grabbing a cookie, sitting down with a book or romcom, relaxing, and sometimes lighting a candle.”  

  

Another Grade 11 student, Ava Trifunovic, states, “Valentine’s Day's meaning is centered around buying things for people, and it’s become a cash grab where corporations just slap hearts on stuff to sell.” 

  
 
A Grade 10 student says “Valentine’s Day means showing love in many ways to people you care about.”  

  

From the olden days to now, Valentine’s Day has evolved and grown from a singular festival to a day that can have different meanings to different people. It’s a day that can be whatever you want it to be. And so, once again, February will drift and sway like a pendulum — swinging around and back next year.
