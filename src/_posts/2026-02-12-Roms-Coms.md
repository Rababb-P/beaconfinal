**Ah, rom-coms.** The Valentine’s Day staple for many people, providing a fantasy to delve in to distract them from their own love life (or lack thereof). The rom-com is a classic genre, given its romantic and comedic appeal, and 90s/2000s rom coms are honestly fantastic. The witty dialogue, iconic characters, and genuinely interesting love stories make for a nostalgic viewing experience every time I watch movies like *Clueless* (1995) or *How to Lose a Guy in 10 Days* (2003), but these movies have unfortunately caused what I like to call a “Character Archetype Phenomenon”, or CAP for short.  

  
The CAP is essentially  the film industry’s way of manufacturing nostalgia. You loved Kat’s tempestuous behaviour in *10 Things I Hate About You* (1999) so let’s create the “not like other girls” archetype, which eventually led to the “manic pixie dream girl,” present in almost every 2010s rom-com. The womanizing,  

charismatic Alex in Hitch (2005) as well as many other haughty men before him (i.e. Mr. Darcy) led to hundreds of “bad boys” gracing the silver screen. And don’t even get me started on love triangles.  

  

As the years have progressed, and more and more films have fallen victim to the CAP, rom-coms have almost become the loser of the film genres. The occasional gem like To *All the Boys I’ve Loved Before* (2018) only comes once in a blue moon, and while it was able to evolve their characters past an unlikable stereotype (I love you Lara Jean), most can't. While I’m glad bad movies aren’t being praised by the masses, it’s seriously unfortunate how this genre, once loved by many, has become a mere regurgitation of every decent movie before it. With many new rom-coms and book adaptations set to come out in 2026— *Wuthering Heights, You, Me & Tuscany, The People We Meet On Vacation,* etc.—I certainly hope the genre is able to redeem itself, and highlight real people instead of archetypes.
