*PUBLISHED: OCTOBER 9, 2002*  

  
*Editor’s Note: In honour of MCI’s 60th anniversary, the Beacon will be publishing a series of older articles that offer a glimpse back in time to the Martingrove days of yore…  Enjoy this editorial!*  

  
**Martingrove has many endearing qualities: intelligent students, understanding teachers, a wide range of clubs and organizations, and… orange and brown as its school colours.** Now, most would argue that our school's colours are not one of its best attributes, but this writer begs to differ. The rich brown and vibrant orange are simply misunderstood.  

  
It is rumoured that Martingrove got stuck with brown and orange, but there is no evidence that this is true. The person who chose our wonderful colours had a vision of what Martingrove would become, and felt that orange and brown would be an accurate representation. And indeed, they are.  

  
Bright, energetic and brilliant are adjectives that accurately describe the orange we see every day at school. Coincidentally, those same adjectives can be used to describe Martingrove students. Perhaps the person who chose orange knew that the students would embody all the same qualities as the colour. Maybe orange is a better colour than we think.  

  
But what of the brown? The simplest explanation is to think not of mud, but rather of soil, where plants grow. MCI can be considered the soil and we, the students, the plants. Just as the seed of education is planted, so it is at MCI that students broaden their experiences and thrive intellectually. The brown represents the potential for growth and academic flourishing.  

  
On the surface, brown and orange may not be overly appealing. It is knowing what they stand for that gives them their unique beauty. Perhaps it is time we acknowledged this beauty and begin to appreciate our marvellous brown and orange, much the way we enjoy the bright colours of a crisp autumn day.
