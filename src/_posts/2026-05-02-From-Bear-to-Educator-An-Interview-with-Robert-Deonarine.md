**Over the years, Robert Deonarine has accumulated a combined 24 years of experience in the secondary school environment:** five of those as a Martingrove student, and the rest as a dedicated health and physical education teacher.

  

Fortunately for Robert, his teaching career has taken him to schools with more sunlight than what us Bears are accustomed to, having taught at Applewood Acres SS, Brampton Centennial SS, and Darcel Avenue Sr. PS, before settling at Central Peel SS, where he has worked for the past 14 years. From within the walls of the school’s gymnasium, he has taught, coached, and mentored countless students across a range of sports, including volleyball, wrestling, soccer, and badminton—the same sports he once fiercely participated in at MCI until his graduation in 2002, which was also the same year he won the title of Martingrove’s Athlete of the Year. His favourites: wrestling and volleyball; the former saw his team win city champions, while the latter took his team to provincials at OFSAAAAA.

  

As a determined young athlete and former executive of the SAC and BAA (now known as BAC),  Robert’s passionate involvement in school life directed him into a career in education. Over his years of experience, he has found that teaching has changed dramatically—from the rise of the internet to increasing parental expectations, the profession has never been more demanding as it is today.

  

“Students have difficulty paying attention, cellphones and social media [are] everywhere...The least favourable part of the job is the amount of paperwork and decisions being made without any input from the front line workers.”

  

Educating a generation with shrinking attention spans while catering to exhaustive expectations can make teaching a difficult test. But despite the ever-growing hindrances in teaching, the best part of the job still hasn’t changed: the people. Robert finds himself lucky to work alongside those with drive and motivation, students and colleagues alike. “[Teaching] is meaningful especially when you are working with like-minded colleagues and students who wish to pursue excellence.”

  

At the same time, Robert takes on the challenge of pushing students who may not aspire such a level of “excellence,”  learners who do not entirely wish to be in his classes or those that “lack a sense of self-awareness and motivation.” “Constant reminders and building those relationships with all students are important to overcome those hurdles,” says Robert when it comes to motivating his students. Ultimately, life is all about resilience, which is a lesson Robert wishes to be remembered by.

  

The mentorship role of a teacher can be paramount to one’s future. It was certainly so for Robert, whose teachers at MCI empowered him to find a life in teaching. Mr. Wodchis, Ms. St. Hilaire, and Mr. Karrandjas are a handful of influential figures who Robert credits in his teaching journey. Ultimately, he remembers them for their “mentorship and kind nature,” noting that strong mentorship is founded upon “patience, understanding, and having a sense of direction.”

  

“Keep trying, you will make mistakes, you will fail, however it's a matter of what you will do when you encounter failure. Do you get back up and keep trying or do you give up?” is wise advice that Robert wishes to pass onto current MCI students. “It's all up to you.”
