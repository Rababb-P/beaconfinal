**With a new season of sports quickly approaching**, four of MCI’s most admired teachers sat down with me to reveal their coaching endeavours for the 25/26 season.  
  
  
Our beloved MCI Cheer team has been fully rebranded. Mr. Farrow is taking the team down a more *Shakespearean* route, combining his love of Renaissance poetry with stunning flips and jumps. He believes that using 16th-century insults (think “harlot” and “lily-livered”) will “rile up our opponents and get them off their groove.” In my opinion, his approach to Cheer is risky, but if he can pull off “[basing] all our cheer movements on Shaggy's walk,” the team may turn out to be more than just a comedy of errors.  
  
  
Whether you were taught by her, watched her conduct Winter Music Night, or had the misfortune of walking past her class when a student dropped their instrument, all can agree: Ms. Chang is a powerful force. It seems fitting that such a strong individual would coach Martingrove’s brand-new Varsity Football team! Her approach to coaching will be to play it safe, running typical football drills such as onset exercises and long tones. In our interview, she also stressed the importance of going back to the basics, “making sure each member has the fundamental skills down, like scales and lip slurs.” With her grit and attention to detail, MCI’s Varsity Football team will surely succeed in the coming season.  
  
  
While there are many new faces emerging in the coaching world next season, a familiar one is reappearing on a shockingly different team. MCI’s Ultimate Frisbee coach, Mr. Nigro, has been traded to the wrestling team! His coaching style is, of course, rooted in mathematics. However, the addition of math seems to have resulted in the subtraction of another factor: rules. "The school will forgo the typical weight classes the sport has and instead have two teams, A and B, that students can try out for." Mr. Nigro also promised heavy penalties for any player who refuses to pass to girls on the team. When pressed on the fact that there is no "passing" in wrestling, Mr. Nigro mumbled under his breath and seemed to ignore the question.  
  
  
Our final new coach of the 25/26 season is known to anyone involved in Martingrove’s athletics programs. Coming from years of hockey expertise, Mr. Vujacic initially aimed to coach Tackle Football, but “found out that helmets cost hundreds of dollars,” and decided to just create “MCI's newest sport, Tackle Rhythmic Gymnastics! Imagine tackle football, made up of pyramids of athletes stacked on top of each other. Grace, agility, strength and power would determine the top performers.” While some may deem this sport unsafe, Mr. Vujacic offers a different perspective: “Since there would be so many injuries on this team, due to students falling from great heights, it would necessitate a HUGE number of athletes, hence increasing participation levels.” While he may not have helmets, protective gear, or any teams to compete against, Mr. Vujacic has stayed determined in his mission, and I’m sure he’ll make MCI proud.
