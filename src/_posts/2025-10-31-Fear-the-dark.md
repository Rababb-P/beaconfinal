**Even in a world lit by smartphones, street lights, and glowing screens, the dark still has a powerful grip on our imaginations.** But why, in 2025, are we still afraid of something as simple as the absence of light?  

   
This fear originates from centuries ago, when, long before electricity, nightfall meant real danger for humans. Without the ability to see, we had to rely on instincts to protect ourselves from predators or threats.  

  
 The fear of the dark helped us survive. While we no longer sleep in caves or fend off wild animals, that instinct hasn’t gone away. Rather, it’s instilled in us, waiting for the lights to go out.  

   
 But survival alone isn’t what makes us fear the dark. It’s also about the unknown. Not being able to see opens up our imagination and prepares us to expect the worst. Ordinary shapes blur into strange ones. Small movements sound like loud creaks. Our minds fill in the blanks with endless possibilities. Ghosts. Intruders. Things we can’t quite name.  

   
 Additionally, our fears are backed up by the stories and culture we grew up in. From horror films to Halloween traditions, we’ve been influenced with the idea that the night causes spooky events to occur. We tell scary stories at bonfires and visit haunted houses, not because we actually expect something to happen, but because the fear we feel is strangely exciting.  

   
 So why do we still fear the dark? Because no matter how much light we surround ourselves with, the dark remains one of the few unknowns. And sometimes it’s not what’s there, but what might be there that scares us the most.
