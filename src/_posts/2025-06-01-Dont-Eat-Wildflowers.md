**I guess we all got what we wanted last winter: that is, a *real* winter.** “Thank you, Mother Earth,” we must all say in unison.  

  
Perhaps our incessant wishes for a ‘real winter' were a bit too incessant, because it's only recently that the season’s wrath has finally wrapped up. And once again, the birds are chirping, the flowers are blossoming… flowers are blossoming…  

I like that the flowers are blossoming again, and I suppose they *look* gorgeous, don't they? Certainly. But here's something you probably didn't know about them: they taste absolutely AWFUL.  

You're probably thinking to yourself, “why would anyone try to eat a flower?” or “wasn't the April Fools’ issue three months ago?”. Folks, make no mistake, this is not a joke. Flower consumption is, surprisingly, quite the phenomenon.  And in today’s political landscape, two types of flowers can be sourced: edible garnishes or wildflowers, the latter being undeserving of any positive culinary recognition.  



How can I say this? Well, I spent a weekend hunting for wildflowers and came home with some dandelions and pansies to taste.  


Interesting foods can be scarce sometimes, but these weeds are awfully abundant; most of all, the dandelion. And sorry, there's really nothing kind to say about them. They're incredibly—nay, fiercely—nay,revoltingly—bitter. With a raw leafy vegetable texture, they could be in a salad, given that it’s drenched in dressing to disguise the bitterness, although even that might not be enough to save it.  

The pansy leaves had a powerful taste of green—like, #00FF00 green. I L-O-V-E bold flavours, but these were too much for me to handle. Contrary to the dandelions, they did have a slight, pleasant sweetness, but the floralness was too overpowering. What’s a positive comment that I could make… maybe that they’re vegan?
	Flowers are dreadful. There, I said it. Sue me. Deplatform me. But that begs the question: *why would anybody want to eat these things?*  



 I don't know, but these unpalatable nuisances do have a following. While writing this article, I came across an Instagram Reel of an American woman eating flowers just like me, except she really enjoyed them. That begs the next question: *is their economy really that bad right now?* Anything for views (or readers), I suppose. But I should acknowledge, our palettes are so interesting. What I detest is adored by another, what I critique is appreciated by another. For centuries, flowers and plants have been an integral part of many global cuisines and have been eaten for generations. So who am I to say anything about these special ingredients? Well, as the professional in the room, they’re terrible.  

At least summertime is upon us. “Thank you, Mother Earth,” we should all again say in unison. Let's be sure to get outdoors, and perhaps gaze at some flowers—let’s just keep them in the soil.  

 
**Flowers rating: 0/5**
